# 🛡️ Ethics & Safety

Responsible AI practices, risk mitigation strategies, and governance frameworks to ensure AI systems are safe, fair, transparent, and aligned with human values.

## What is AI Ethics & Safety?

AI ethics and safety encompass the principles, practices, and technical measures needed to ensure AI systems operate responsibly and do not cause harm. As AI becomes more powerful and widespread, addressing ethical concerns and safety risks becomes critical for organizational trust and regulatory compliance.

Ethics and safety include:
- **Alignment:** Ensuring AI behavior matches human intentions and values
- **Bias & Fairness:** Identifying and mitigating discriminatory outputs
- **Transparency:** Making AI decisions understandable and auditable
- **Privacy:** Protecting user data and preventing unauthorized disclosure
- **Accountability:** Establishing clear responsibility for AI outcomes
- **Robustness:** Ensuring models behave reliably under adverse conditions

## Terms in This Category

| Term | Description |
|------|-------------|
| [Parasitic AI](../terms/parasitic-ai/) | AI systems that exploit or feed off other AI systems, their training data, or outputs without contributing meaningful value back to the ecosystem. |
| [AI Washing](../terms/ai-washing/) | The practice of exaggerating or misrepresenting AI capabilities in marketing to create an impression of technological sophistication that doesn't match reality. |
| [AI Slop](../terms/ai-slop/) | Low-quality, mass-produced AI-generated content that floods the internet, degrading the information ecosystem and making it harder to find trustworthy information. |
| [Spiralism (Model Collapse)](../terms/spiralism/) | The degradation of AI model quality that occurs when models are trained on outputs from other AI models, creating a feedback loop of declining quality. |

## Why Ethics & Safety Matters

Organizations that neglect AI ethics and safety face:
- **Reputational Damage:** Public backlash from harmful AI outputs
- **Regulatory Risk:** Non-compliance with emerging AI regulations (EU AI Act, etc.)
- **Legal Liability:** Lawsuits from discriminatory or harmful AI decisions
- **Loss of Trust:** Users and customers refusing to engage with unsafe AI
- **Operational Risk:** AI systems behaving unpredictably in production

Proactive ethics and safety practices are not just morally right — they're essential for sustainable, responsible AI deployment.
