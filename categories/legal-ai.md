# ⚖️ Legal AI

AI terminology, regulatory frameworks, and legal principles governing AI systems, focusing on compliance, liability, and the protection of fundamental rights.

## What is Legal AI?

Legal AI encompasses the laws, regulations, and legal frameworks that dictate how AI systems can be developed, deployed, and used. As AI becomes more powerful, governments worldwide are establishing strict legal boundaries to ensure safety, protect privacy, and prevent discrimination.

Legal AI includes:
- **Regulatory Frameworks:** Laws like the EU AI Act that define risk categories and compliance requirements.
- **Liability & Accountability:** Determining who is legally responsible when an AI system causes harm.
- **Intellectual Property:** Navigating copyright and patent issues related to AI-generated content and training data.
- **Fundamental Rights:** Ensuring AI does not violate privacy, enable discrimination, or facilitate unauthorized surveillance.

## Terms in This Category

| Term | Description |
|------|-------------|
| [AI Act](/ai-dictionary/terms/ai-act/) | The European Union's comprehensive, risk-based regulatory framework for AI, establishing strict rules for high-risk systems and banning unacceptable practices. |
| [AI Copyright & IP](/ai-dictionary/terms/ai-copyright-ip/) | The evolving legal landscape surrounding intellectual property rights for AI-generated works and the use of copyrighted material in training datasets. |
| [AI Forensics (Media Forensics)](/ai-dictionary/terms/ai-forensics/) | The scientific discipline of analyzing digital media to detect AI manipulation, deepfakes, and synthetic content for legal and investigative purposes. |
| [AI-Generated Evidence](/ai-dictionary/terms/ai-generated-evidence/) | Digital evidence created or processed by AI systems, raising complex legal questions regarding authenticity, admissibility, and chain of custody in court. |
| [AI Liability](/ai-dictionary/terms/ai-liability/) | The legal frameworks determining who is held legally and financially responsible when an AI system causes harm, damage, or injury. |
| [Algorithmic Accountability](/ai-dictionary/terms/algorithmic-accountability/) | The principle that organizations must be held responsible for the outcomes and impacts of their AI systems, including providing mechanisms for redress. |
| [Algorithmic Audit](/ai-dictionary/terms/algorithmic-audit/) | An independent, systematic evaluation of an AI system's design, data, and outcomes to ensure compliance with legal, ethical, and performance standards. |
| [Algorithmic Risk Assessment](/ai-dictionary/terms/algorithmic-risk-assessment/) | A mandatory evaluation process to identify and mitigate potential harms, biases, and security vulnerabilities in an AI system before deployment. |
| [Automated Decision-Making (ADM)](/ai-dictionary/terms/automated-decision-making/) | The practice of making legal or significant decisions about individuals solely through AI algorithms, often subject to strict regulatory restrictions and rights to human review. |
| [Conformity Assessment](/ai-dictionary/terms/conformity-assessment/) | The formal, legally mandated evaluation process required to verify that a high-risk AI system complies with all applicable regulatory requirements before deployment. |
| [eDiscovery](/ai-dictionary/terms/ediscovery/) | The legal process of identifying, collecting, and producing electronically stored information (ESI) in response to litigation or regulatory investigations, increasingly augmented by AI. |
| [Generative AI Disclosure](/ai-dictionary/terms/generative-ai-disclosure/) | Legal and regulatory requirements mandating that users be informed when they are interacting with an AI system or consuming AI-generated content. |
| [High-Risk AI System](/ai-dictionary/terms/high-risk-ai-system/) | AI applications classified by regulators (e.g., EU AI Act) as posing significant risks to health, safety, or fundamental rights, subject to strict compliance and conformity assessments. |
| [Impact Assessment](/ai-dictionary/terms/impact-assessment/) | A formal evaluation required before deploying high-risk AI to assess its potential effects on fundamental rights, privacy, and societal well-being. |
| [Prohibited AI Practices](/ai-dictionary/terms/prohibited-ai-practices/) | AI applications deemed to pose an unacceptable risk to fundamental rights and are therefore strictly banned by law, such as real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces or social scoring systems. |
| [Regulatory Sandbox](/ai-dictionary/terms/regulatory-sandbox/) | A controlled, supervised environment established by regulators where companies can test innovative AI systems with relaxed regulatory constraints before full market deployment. |
| [Right to Explanation](/ai-dictionary/terms/right-to-explanation/) | A legal right (e.g., under GDPR) granting individuals the ability to demand a meaningful explanation of the logic behind an automated decision that affects them. |
| [Systemic Risk](/ai-dictionary/terms/systemic-risk/) | The potential for advanced, highly capable AI models to cause widespread, society-level harm, including threats to democratic processes, public safety, or critical infrastructure. |
| [TAR (Technology-Assisted Review)](/ai-dictionary/terms/tar-technology-assisted-review/) | The use of machine learning and AI to prioritize and classify large volumes of documents during the legal eDiscovery process, significantly reducing manual review time. |
| [Trustworthy AI](/ai-dictionary/terms/trustworthy-ai/) | A legal and ethical framework ensuring AI systems are lawful, ethical, and robust throughout their entire lifecycle to maintain public trust. |

## Why Legal AI Matters

Navigating the legal landscape of AI is no longer optional for organizations. Legal AI considerations impact:
- **Market Access:** Non-compliance with frameworks like the EU AI Act can result in massive fines and product bans.
- **Litigation Risk:** Clear liability frameworks are essential to protect organizations from lawsuits arising from AI errors.
- **Consumer Trust:** Legal compliance and transparency are foundational to building and maintaining user trust.
- **Innovation Pace:** Regulatory sandboxes and clear guidelines help organizations innovate confidently within legal boundaries.

Understanding legal AI ensures organizations can deploy powerful technologies while remaining compliant, ethical, and legally protected.
